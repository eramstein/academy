# Event: An Invitation to the Workshop

**Event ID:** `frank_artifact_workshop_intro`  
**Trigger:** Day 5, morning
**Emotion:** Neutral
**Character:** frank_artifact_master

## Text

The morning's artificery lesson has barely begun when a tremendous clatter erupts from the corridor.

Professor Lombard stops mid-sentence. A metallic crash follows, then a booming voice.

"Nothing broken! Nothing important, anyway!"

The classroom door swings open, admitting a mountain of a man in a battered leather apron. His sleeves are rolled above enormous forearms, his thick black beard is dusted with soot, and a collection of tools jangles from his belt. He carries a curious brass contraption under one arm and wears the broad, cheerful grin of someone having an exceptionally good morning.

"Morning, Professor! Borrow your students for a minute?"

Lombard regards the visitor, then the faint trail of oily footprints he has left across the floor.

"Provided you don't dismantle anything, Frank."

"Wouldn't dream of it!"

Frank deposits his contraption on the nearest desk. It unfolds with a series of clicks into a delicate arrangement of brass rings surrounding a blue crystal. The crystal begins to glow.

"Right, then! Name's Frank. I run the Academy workshop, down by the old foundry. Some of you have probably heard the hammering. Those who haven't should consider having their ears examined."

A few students laugh.

"Now, you're learning to make cards in here, and fine things they are. But artificery doesn't begin and end with a bit of enchanted paper. There's a whole world of useful things you can build with your own two hands!"

He taps the brass contraption affectionately.

"**Artifacts.** Tools, totems, trinkets. A good tool can help you craft better cards. A well-made totem can give you an advantage during a match. And a clever little trinket? Well, you'd be surprised how often the right contraption can get you out of trouble."

He gives the crystal a twist. The brass rings spin faster, producing a pleasant musical hum.

"Of course, making these things takes more than a strong arm and a hammer. You'll need to understand the magical colors you're working with. Pay attention in your lessons, study hard, learn from your classmates. The more you know, the more ambitious your creations can become."

The humming abruptly becomes a high-pitched whistle.

Frank frowns at the contraption.

"Ah. That's new."

He gives it a firm tap. The whistling stops.

"There we are! Now, I've got a few simple patterns ready for beginners. Nothing too fancy, but enough to get you started. You can come by the workshop whenever you like, pick a pattern, and have a go at making something."

He raises a thick, grease-stained finger.

"**One rule, though: you bring your own materials.** I'll provide the benches, the tools, and whatever advice I can shout at you. But iron, copper, crystals, and the rest? That's your responsibility. Can't have you lot emptying my stores every time you get an idea."

"An eminently sensible policy," Lombard observes.

"Had to introduce it after the last lot," Frank replies darkly. "You wouldn't believe how much brass a determined student can waste."

He folds the contraption back into its casing, tucks it under his arm, and heads for the door.

"Anyway, you're all welcome! Don't worry if you've never held a hammer. We'll soon put that right. And remember: the first rule of engineering is that if something doesn't work, you've learned one way not to build it!"

He pauses in the doorway.

"Second rule: if it starts ticking and you didn't put a clock in it, come and fetch me."

With a hearty laugh, he disappears into the corridor.

Professor Lombard surveys the class, waits for the last of the chuckles to subside, and returns to the blackboard.

"Now, where were we? Ah, yes. The importance of precise measurements."

You glance at the door.

The workshop certainly sounds worth a visit.
