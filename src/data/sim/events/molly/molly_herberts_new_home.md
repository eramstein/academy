Text
The cafeteria is almost deserted when you arrive. A few students linger over their supper, and somewhere in the kitchen, someone is conducting a noisy disagreement with a collection of pots.

You're considering whether anything edible might have survived the evening rush when you notice Molly sitting alone on a bench near the window.

She's crying.

Not loudly. Just quiet, miserable little sniffles, punctuated by attempts to wipe her cheeks with her sleeve. She's holding something tightly against her chest, wrapped in a piece of green cloth.

You hurry over.

"Molly? What's wrong?"

She looks up, her eyes red. "Oh, Antonio..."

Something moves in her arms.

You look closer.

A tiny brown rabbit pokes its nose out of the cloth, twitches its whiskers, and regards you with the serene indifference of a creature entirely unaware that it has ruined someone's evening.

"This is Herbert," Molly explains, stroking his ears. "He's my rabbit."

"Hello, Herbert."

Herbert sniffs your finger. Apparently, you pass inspection.

"I've had him for years," Molly continues. "He's such a good little fellow. He never makes any trouble, and he hardly eats anything, and he always sleeps beside my pillow..."

Her voice trembles.

"But Mrs. Mathilda found him in my room at the inn today. She was furious! She said she'd told me a hundred times there were no pets allowed. Which isn't true. She's only told me three times."

You begin to suspect that Mrs. Mathilda may have a point, at least regarding the accuracy of Molly's interpretation of house rules.

"She said if Herbert isn't gone by tonight, we'll both have to leave."

Molly hugs the rabbit closer.

"I can't afford another room, Antonio. And I can't just abandon him! He's never lived outside. He doesn't know how to look after himself."

A tear rolls down her cheek. Herbert stretches up and licks it.

"Oh, Herbert," she whispers.

Well. There are problems that can be solved with rigorous reasoning, problems that can be solved with magic, and problems that involve a crying girl and a rabbit.

This one may require a different approach.

"Herbert isn't very good at being a rabbit," Molly says miserably. "He once got frightened by a lettuce."

You do your best to reassure her. You talk about green grass, warm sunshine, and the freedom to hop wherever his little heart desires.

Eventually, Molly manages a small smile.

"I suppose you're right. Perhaps he'd be happier with other rabbits."

She strokes Herbert's ears for a long moment.

"Thank you, Antonio. I know you're trying to help."

She leans against your shoulder, and you stay with her until her tears have dried.

Herbert, meanwhile, has fallen asleep.

At least one member of this conversation has achieved perfect peace of mind.
