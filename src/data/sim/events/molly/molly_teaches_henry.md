# Event: The Teacher Becomes the Student

**Event ID:** `molly_teaches_henry`  
**Invitation:** Molly seems to be giving Henry a lesson in the library.  
**Trigger:** evening, starting from day 7.
**Location:** Library  
**Type:** Optional event

## Text

You find Molly and Henry sitting at a library table, surrounded by textbooks, loose papers, and several playing cards arranged in suspiciously military formations.

"...so the green essence reinforces the structure, while the blue determines how it behaves," Molly explains, moving two cards together. "Think of it like wrestling. Green is how hard you can hold someone down. Blue is knowing where to put your hands."

Henry nods thoughtfully. "Oh! I think I understand."

You stop in your tracks. Molly? Teaching artificery? The same Molly who recently confessed that Professor Glützenbaum's lectures made her feel like her brain was being tied into increasingly elaborate knots?

Molly notices you and waves, looking simultaneously pleased and embarrassed.

"Hello, Antonio! Henry asked me about essence combinations, and, well, I thought I remembered enough to help." She glances at her notes. "I'm not entirely sure I'm explaining it properly, though."

"You're explaining it much better than the textbook," Henry offers quietly.

Molly beams at him, then looks at you expectantly.

## Options

### Option A: Help her become a better teacher

You suggest that Molly try explaining the principles without looking at her notes. If she can explain them to Henry in her own words, she'll probably understand them better herself.

**Text:**

Molly looks doubtful. "Without the notes? Antonio, that's rather like asking me to wrestle blindfolded."

"Have you ever tried?"

"Well, yes, actually. But that's different."

Nevertheless, she accepts the challenge. You help her organize her thoughts, asking simple questions whenever she gets stuck and encouraging her to find her own explanations rather than repeating the textbook.

Gradually, the pauses become shorter, the explanations clearer. Henry listens attentively, occasionally asking questions that force Molly to think a little harder.

By the end of the session, she closes her textbook with a look of genuine satisfaction.

"You know, I think I finally understand this properly. Not just the words, but what they actually _mean_."

She gives you a warm smile. "Thank you, Antonio. I don't think I'd have managed that on my own."

Herbert is becoming quite the expert in artificery. Although I suspect he only listens because I feed him carrots afterward."

### Option B: Trust Molly's instincts

You tell Molly that her explanations are perfectly good, and encourage her to stop second-guessing herself. Henry clearly understands what she's saying. Why not let her finish?

**Text:**

"But what if I tell him something wrong?" Molly asks.

"Then he'll be no worse off than half the students who've attended Glützenbaum's lectures."

Henry makes a small, startled noise that might be a laugh.

Molly hesitates, then turns back to her improvised battlefield of cards. She begins explaining another principle, this time with considerably more enthusiasm. Her analogies grow increasingly elaborate, involving grappling holds, opposing forces, and one particularly unfortunate imaginary wrestler who appears to violate several laws of natural philosophy.

Henry follows every word.

You watch Molly gradually forget that she's supposed to be nervous. She's animated, confident, and clearly enjoying herself.

When Henry finally leaves, Molly turns to you with a thoughtful expression.

"I suppose I do know a few things after all."

"More than a few."

She grins. "Don't tell Emma. She'll expect me to start doing my homework properly."

**Outcomes:**

- Increase `molly_plot_academic_struggle` by 1.
- Molly respect +2.
- Add memory `molly_memory_unexpected_teacher`, known by molly: Molly gives you a mischievous smile. "I've discovered something wonderful about teaching, Antonio. When you're the one explaining things, nobody knows which bits you're making up." She laughs. "I'm joking! Mostly. But it really is easier when I stop worrying about sounding clever."

### Option C: Turn the lesson into a game

You suggest a friendly competition: Molly must explain three artificery principles using nothing but sporting analogies, while Henry has to identify the actual magical principle. You'll serve as referee.

**Text:**

Molly's eyes light up immediately.

"Oh, I like this!"

Henry looks less certain. "Is there going to be actual wrestling?"

"Only if you get the answers wrong," Molly replies solemnly.

The first round goes well. The second involves a complicated comparison between magical resistance and an opponent attempting to escape a headlock. By the third, Molly has cleared half the table and is demonstrating the principle of opposing forces with a pair of rolled-up manuscripts.

Henry, ordinarily so reserved, is laughing openly. He even offers a sporting analogy of his own, which Molly declares excellent despite its unfortunate suggestion that a goalkeeper might be an appropriate component in an enchantment.

Eventually the librarian casts a pointed glance in your direction, and the three of you hastily restore the manuscripts to their proper condition.

"That was fun," Henry says, gathering his books. "Could we do it again sometime?"

"Of course!" Molly replies, delighted.

She turns to you with a broad grin. "See, Antonio? Studying doesn't have to be miserable. We should have thought of this ages ago!"
