Event: A Letter with a Seal
NPC: molly
Event ID: molly_letter_with_seal
Trigger: Day >= 12; Molly friendship >= 3
Image: medium
Emotion: surprised
Text
Molly is making her way through an Academy corridor with a basket balanced on one arm and a stack of books pressed against her chest. A loaf of sweet bread protrudes from the basket at a precarious angle. She greets you enthusiastically, which proves too much for the entire arrangement.

One book slides free. Then another. The bread makes a bid for independence. You catch the basket just as a heavy envelope flutters to the floor.

The envelope is thick cream paper, folded with almost military precision and closed by an imposing red wax seal. A coat of arms is pressed into it, surrounded by enough flourishes to suggest that the sender has never written an informal note in their life. Across the front, in impeccable calligraphy, is Molly's name—accompanied by an impressive procession of titles.

"Oh! My letter." Molly snatches up the envelope, then catches herself. "Sorry, Antonio. I didn't mean to grab it from you. Thank you for saving the cakes!"

She slips the letter between her books, but not before you notice the corner is creased from being opened and folded again. Her sunny expression falters for a moment.

"It's from home," she explains. "They want to know how my studies are going. Whether I've made the proper acquaintances. Whether I'm making progress toward... well, the things I'm supposed to do afterward."

She looks down at her basket. "The cakes are for some of the first-years. They looked terribly miserable after the alchemy test. I was going to cheer them up."

Apparently the first-years' spirits are an urgent matter. The weight of a family dynasty is rather less convenient to carry around in a basket.

  Options
  Option A: Respect her privacy
  Text:

"You don't have to explain," you tell her, handing back the books. "It looks like a private letter."

Molly's shoulders relax. "Thank you. It's not a secret, exactly. It's just... complicated."

She hesitates, studying the seal. "Everyone at home is very certain about what would make me useful. I suppose they're trying to look after me."

You let the subject rest. Molly places the letter safely beneath her books, then offers you a pastry with the solemn gratitude of someone bestowing a medal.

"Here. For rescuing the basket. And for not asking questions I don't quite know how to answer yet."

  Option B: Offer to help with her reply
  Text:

"Would it help if we worked on a reply together? Not the private parts. Just the part about your studies."

Molly considers this with immediate relief. "Oh, that would be wonderful! I always get stuck trying to sound... important."

You suggest beginning with what she has actually been doing: lessons attended, exercises completed, people met. Molly counts these on her fingers, then adds, "And I helped some children with their reading! Although I don't suppose that counts as academic progress."

"Why shouldn't it? You've learned enough to teach somebody else."

"Oh! That's true, isn't it?" Her face brightens. "I never thought of putting it that way."

She pulls a little notebook from her basket and begins jotting down ideas. After a moment, she pauses. "I still want to tell them the truth. I'm not getting top marks. And I haven't decided what I want to do afterward."

"Then that's what the letter should say."

"Yes," Molly says, quietly but firmly. "I think it should."

The draft, when she reads it back to you, is less grand than the envelope. It is also unmistakably hers.

  Option C: Admire the magnificently pompous stationery
  Text:

You gesture toward the envelope peeking out between her books. "Molly, I had no idea one could fit an entire family history into a blob of wax. Do they send invitations to breakfast by royal proclamation?"

Molly takes the envelope and peers at it seriously. "Well, breakfast invitations are usually on smaller paper."

You look at her. She looks at you. Then she realizes what you mean and starts laughing.

"Oh, Antonio! No, not every breakfast! Only when we have guests." She pauses. "That sounded worse, didn't it?"

"A little."

"I once tried to send my brother a letter with a drawing of a duck instead of a proper seal," she confesses. "The household secretary sent it back because he thought someone had tampered with the correspondence."

You suggest that the duck may have been insufficiently heraldic. Molly laughs so hard she has to put the basket down before the pastries make a second escape.

"I know they're trying to be proper," she says when she catches her breath. "But sometimes I wish they'd just write, 'Dear Molly, how are you?'"
