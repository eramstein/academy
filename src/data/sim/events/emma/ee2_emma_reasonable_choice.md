Text

You recognize Emma before you recognize the dress.

She is coming down the Academy steps as the last of the evening light catches the stone. Dark, lustrous fabric falls elegantly from her shoulders; a fine necklace glimmers against her skin. Her hair is arranged with the sort of care that suggests a considerable investment of time and several rejected alternatives. The effect is breathtaking—and quite deliberate. Emma Delamain has never believed in leaving an important occasion to chance.

She notices you and stops. For the briefest moment, she looks almost relieved. Then the familiar, composed smile returns.

"Antonio. Good evening."

"You look..."

"Yes," she says, smoothing an entirely imaginary crease. "I rather hoped I would."

Her smile is correct in every particular. It does not quite reach her eyes.

"I'm on my way to dinner with Lucien de Varenne. You remember him?"

"He has invited me to a private dinner at his family's residence. Just the two of us, this time." She pauses. "It seems he would like us to become better acquainted. Personally, I mean."

You take another look at the dress. Of course.

"And?" you ask.

"And I accepted."

She delivers the words like the conclusion of a properly constructed proof. A moment later, she begins supplying the premises without being asked.

"Lucien is intelligent, well educated, considerate. His work is genuinely interesting. He understands what I want to accomplish, and his family has the connections to help me accomplish it. More importantly, he seems to admire me for my work rather than merely for—" She gestures impatiently at her dress. "Well. You understand."

"He sounds impressive."

"He is. And generous without being condescending, which is rarer than it ought to be." She looks toward the gate. "I have spent years trying to build precisely this kind of future. Respectable work. Security. Someone beside me whose ambitions are compatible with mine. The sort of life in which my parents need never worry again."

"It's almost embarrassingly sensible," she says, with a small attempt at a laugh. "You know how I detest an untidy solution."

She adjusts one of her gloves, although it is already perfectly straight. Her eyes return to yours, linger, then dart away.

"Well?" she asks. "You're unusually quiet, Fibonacci. What do you think?"

Title: A Question of Judgment
Emotion: angry, then sad

"Let us examine that suggestion," Emma says, enunciating each word with clinical precision. "Lucien is a mature man, established in his profession, accomplished in his field. He has a remarkable education, impeccable manners, considerable resources, and a clear idea of what he wants from life. He is kind. He takes my work seriously. He has offered me nothing I have not earned, and he has never once asked me to become smaller to fit beside him."

She folds her hands. You recognize the posture: the beginning of an oral examination for which you are disastrously underprepared.

"And you, Antonio, are several years younger than I am. You are talented, yes. Promising. Sometimes astonishingly so. But you have barely begun. You have no established position, no certainty about your future, and a truly bewildering tendency to improvise when a perfectly good plan is available."

"I know. But—"

"But what? What exactly are you offering? That I discard a rare, excellent prospect for someone who still has everything to prove? Because he asks me to?"

You open your mouth. She waits.

"I care about you," you manage. "We work well together. We could build something—"

"Could. An inspiring word. Very popular with people who have no evidence."

"Emma, that's not fair."

"Isn't it?" Her chin lifts. "Then tell me why. Give me a reason, Antonio. One good reason why I should make such an absurdly irrational choice."

You try. You speak of how much you enjoy being with her, of how well you understand each other, of all the things the two of you might yet do. Each argument seems pale beside the polished future she has just described. You run out of words.

Her expression changes. Not triumph. Something much worse.

"Oh, for heaven's sake," she whispers.

And then Emma Delamain, who can face a hall full of professors without trembling and argue an impossible position into respectability, begins to cry.

"No. No, no, no. You hopeless idiot."

She turns away, furious with the tears, and wipes at her cheeks with the back of one glove.

"You're supposed to tell me that those things aren't all you are. You're supposed to remind me that when everyone else saw an ambitious, difficult girl with nothing to her name, you saw me."

"For what it's worth, Antonio... I've never seen her care so much about someone. Try to be careful with that."

You wait until she is gone before opening the letter.

Inside is a single folded sheet. On it, just a few words:

I tried to craft something useful last week, but this is the only thing that would come out. I thought you should know.

— Emma

There is something else in the envelope: a Hordes card, exquisitely made. The colors are luminous, the enchantment delicate and unmistakably hers. In its illustration stand two young adventurers, together. The woman has Emma's familiar proud bearing and fair hair; the man beside her looks very much like you. Neither is looking toward a distant prize or an approaching enemy. They are looking at each other.

For once, there is no clever annotation in the margin. No calculation. No teasing remark carefully positioned to make sincerity deniable.

Only the two of you, as she imagined you might be.

You sit down on the edge of your bed with the card in your hands. You turn it over once, then back again. The room is very quiet.

It seems Emma has finally made her argument.
