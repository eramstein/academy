## Text

Emma is speaking with another student when she catches sight of you. Her sentence falters by half a beat—an event so rare that it probably deserves to be recorded in the Academy archives. She recovers, finishes her conversation with perfect courtesy, and gives you a small, discreet nod toward the courtyard.

You follow her out past the stone arches and the flowerbeds. The courtyard is quiet at this hour. Emma walks as far as a bench beneath an old tree, turns to face you, and opens her mouth.

Nothing comes out.

She folds her hands. Unfolds them. Smooths a sleeve that requires no smoothing.

"Well," she says.

"Well," you agree.

"I thought perhaps we should..."

"Yes?"

"Discuss..." She makes a small, impatient gesture. "Yesterday."

You wait. There is a remarkable pleasure in seeing Emma Delamain, terror of oral examinations and scourge of badly defended arguments, lose a duel against the beginning of a sentence.

Her eyes narrow. Then she laughs—a surprised, delighted laugh that does wonderful things for the state of your nerves.

"Very well," she says. "I walked directly into that one."

"You did. Rather spectacularly."

"Enjoy it, Fibonacci. Opportunities like this are exceptionally rare."

"I intend to have the date engraved on a plaque."

"Make certain you spell my name correctly."

The laughter fades into a comfortable, slightly shy silence. She glances at your hands, then looks you in the eye.

"Antonio, I owe you an apology. A proper one. Yesterday I asked you to do something absurd, and then I became furious when you couldn't do it. I wanted you to produce a brilliant argument on command while I stood there explaining, in considerable detail, why you were an unsuitable candidate."

"It was a remarkably thorough presentation."

"I had prepared the evidence carefully. Unfortunately, I seem to have been arguing against my own position."

She gives a rueful smile.

"You weren't supposed to read my mind. I could have told you what I wanted. I should have. Instead I behaved like a dreadful examiner who hadn't bothered to give her student the questions. And then I..." Her cheeks color. "Well. I rather lost my composure."

"A little."

"Antonio."

"There may have been a few tears. And a minor physical assault."

"Those were _emphatic punctuation marks_."

"I see. I shall inform the faculty."

She shakes her head, smiling in spite of herself.

"I'm sorry I made you feel small. You didn't deserve that."

"I'm sorry I made you cry," you say. "I thought I was asking you to give up a perfect future for a rather uncertain one. I didn't realize I might actually have a chance."

Emma raises an eyebrow. "Yes. Your powers of observation have occasionally proved rather disappointing for a man of your academic promise."

"In my defense, you can be quite intimidating."

"Good. I'd hate to discover that all my effort had been wasted."

Then she looks away, her smile becoming smaller and softer.

"For the record, I wrote to Lucien. He was very gracious. He deserves someone who is genuinely interested in him, rather than someone who finds herself thinking about another man over dinner."

"I'm glad he took it well."

"So am I. He's an excellent man. And I am still interested in the research, if the opportunity remains available. Those things needn't be connected."

"Naturally."

"In fact, I shall probably accomplish something extraordinary in the field, become scandalously successful, and spend my fortune on books and unnecessarily expensive tea."

"A fine plan."

"There is, however, one rather serious unresolved question."

She regards you with an expression that almost manages to be solemn.

"What exactly did you make of my card?"

"You mean the one with the two of us?"

"I believe it was reasonably unambiguous. Though after yesterday, I thought perhaps I should check."

"I thought it was beautiful."

"That wasn't the question."

"I know."

She takes a breath. This time, she does not look away.

"I want to be with you, Antonio. Not because it makes the most sensible plan on paper, and certainly not because you performed adequately at yesterday's examination. You failed that rather decisively." Her smile returns. "I want to be with you because life is more interesting and more joyful when you're in it. And because, against my better judgment, I seem to have fallen quite hopelessly in love with you."

For once, she lets the words stand without immediately rescuing them with a joke.

"There," she says at last. "A clear statement of intent. Remarkably efficient. I ought to have tried it sooner."

She holds your gaze, hopeful and a little nervous, but no longer uncertain about what she wants.

"Now. What do _you_ want?"

## Options

### Option A: Kiss her

**Text:**

"I think," you say, stepping closer, "that we've both spent quite enough time talking."

"An unusual opinion from a scholar."

"I may have discovered a more persuasive form of argument."

"Oh?"

You lean toward her slowly enough to give her every opportunity to object.

She doesn't. Instead, she catches the front of your jacket and kisses you, smiling against your lips. The second kiss is less tentative. When you finally draw apart, she is looking rather pleased with herself.

"Well," she says. "That was a considerable improvement on yesterday's performance."

"Have I passed?"

"Provisionally. There will be regular practical examinations."

"That sounds demanding."

"My standards are notoriously high."

You take her hand. This time, she doesn't need a pretext to keep hold of yours.

"Emma?"

"Yes?"

"I love you."

For a moment, all her clever replies desert her. Then she gives you a smile so open and happy that you wonder how you ever mistook her for someone cold.

"I love you too, you idiot."

She rests her forehead against yours before adding, in a whisper, "And if you tell Molly how embarrassingly sentimental I've become, I shall deny every word."

"Molly already knows."

"Yes. An unfortunate complication. I may have to bribe her."

You leave the courtyard together, hand in hand. There are examinations to prepare for, matches to win, and a rather ambitious future to build. None of those things has changed.

Except that, for once, Emma seems perfectly content not to know precisely how every part of that future will unfold.

### Option B: Ask to remain friends

**Text:**

You look at her for a long moment. There is no easy way to say it, and she deserves better than an evasive answer.

"Emma, you mean more to me than I can put into words. Yesterday made that very clear. But I don't think I can promise you the kind of relationship you're asking for. Not honestly."

She is still. You can see the disappointment arrive, and how hard she works not to conceal it with an easy joke.

"I see," she says quietly.

"I don't want to lose what we have. The friendship, the arguments, the work—any of it. But it wouldn't be fair to say yes because I'm frightened of hurting you."

Emma lowers her gaze to her hands. Then she nods.

"No. It wouldn't. And I have had quite enough of people making decisions on the basis of what they think I would like to hear."

She breathes out slowly.

"For the record, I think you're making a dreadful error of judgment."

"I expected nothing less."

"A truly spectacular error. I may write a paper on it."

"Peer-reviewed?"

"Naturally. Though finding anyone who disagrees with me may prove difficult."

That wins a small laugh from both of you. She looks sad, but she is herself again.

"I may need a little time," she says. "Not because I no longer value your friendship. Quite the opposite. I simply need a chance to arrange my thoughts without making another scene on the Academy steps."

"Of course."

"And Antonio? I don't regret the card. I meant every part of it."

"I'll treasure it."

"You'd better. It took an appalling amount of work."

She touches your arm briefly, then turns toward the Academy. Halfway to the arch, she looks back.

"Don't think this means I'll go easy on you in our next match."

"I wouldn't dream of it."

"Good. Some things, at least, can remain entirely unreasonable."
