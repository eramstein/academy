## Text

"Can you believe them? All airs and graces until someone gives them a proper thrashing, and then their true colors come out! Miserable scoundrels! Blackguards! Unspeakable little weasels!"

Well. You've never seen Emma quite like this. Your impeccably mannered, perpetually composed Emma, apparently equipped with an entire vocabulary of insults you've never heard her use before. You make a mental note never to get on her bad side.

{{flushedCheeks}}

"Don't worry, Emma. We'll figure something out," you assure her, cradling a trophy so enormous that the sight of you carrying it is becoming faintly ridiculous.

"Of course we'll figure something out. But without any money, we'll need to be a little inventive. Perhaps—"

Emma stops. Her gaze settles on a farmer's cart, piled high with vegetables, trundling toward the city gates in the direction of the Academy.

"Ah, yes," she mutters. "The triumphant return of the conquering heroes."

She motions for you to stay put and strides toward the driver.

Half a minute later, she beckons you over. The farmer has apparently agreed to take her along for free. Judging by the look of wonder on his face, this has required remarkably little persuasion.

The wonder fades into resignation when he sees you approaching, trophy and all.

"Right, you two. I'll take you to that school of whatever-it-is, but I've got two conditions. First, no funny business in my cart! I know what you young lovebirds are like."

Emma draws herself up, visibly outraged. Unfortunately for her, you recover first.

"Not to worry, sir. My wife is perfectly capable of controlling her passions. Most of the time."

Emma turns very slowly toward you.

You'll pay for that later. Absolutely worth it.

"And second," the farmer continues, pointing his thumb at a mountainous stack of crates, "you help me load up."

## Node: the_champions_return

**Title:** The Champions' Triumphant Return  
**Image:** `emma_falkenheim_cart.jpg` large  
**Emotion:** amused

"Ah."

You and Emma stare into the back of the cart.

It is packed to the brim with vegetables. There is barely room for two people, let alone two people and a trophy the size of a small child. Emma, who has been gazing at the latter with undisguised affection ever since the award ceremony, suddenly seems rather less enamored of it.

"Would you prefer to sit on the carrots or the potatoes, darling?"

"Oh, give it a rest. He can't hear you anymore."

"I know, my love."

Emma rolls her eyes and climbs aboard.

To your surprise, she reveals a remarkable talent for logistics. Within moments, a few crates have been shifted, several sacks rearranged, and a small, reasonably comfortable nest has emerged between a wall of cabbages and a heap of apples.

She settles into it, draws her knees up, and pats the floor beside her.

The smile on her face suggests that revenge is imminent.

"Don't be shy, sweetheart."

Oh, no.

Forty centimeters. At most.

You swallow and sit down as far from her as physically possible, which turns out to mean pressed firmly against her side, with the enormous trophy balanced across your knees.

"Not going to faint on me, are you, Fibonacci?"

You brace yourself for several hours of merciless teasing.

But, unexpectedly, Emma merely gives you a peaceful little smile. The cart lurches forward, and she turns to watch the countryside roll past.

No sarcastic remarks. No clever observations at your expense.

Well.

Here you are, then.

Lovely weather, isn't it?

And, er...

Oh, for heaven's sake, _say something._

You risk a glance at Emma. She's watching the distant hills, looking almost dreamy. Several minutes pass. She doesn't appear troubled by the silence in the slightest.

Then she turns toward you.

Quick. Think of something.

But she only smiles, very slightly, before looking away again.

Eventually, she takes a book from her bag and begins reading. Every now and then, she comes across a passage she particularly enjoys and reads it aloud to you, supplying a scholarly commentary that is considerably longer than the passage itself.

Naturally, this also presents opportunities to remind you that a little more reading would do you a world of good.

The hours slip by. The afternoon light softens. The sun sinks toward the horizon.

After the madness of the past week, exhaustion begins to catch up with you.

You really ought to stay awake. It would be terribly embarrassing to—

When you open your eyes again, it is dark.

Someone has tucked a blanket around you.

That's... rather nice, actually.

Wait.

Something isn't quite right.

Your head.

Oh.

Very slowly, you lift it. Straighten your back. Fix your eyes on the darkness ahead.

Whatever you do, don't look at her.

The cart rattles to a stop.

"Sleep well?"

Emma is pressing her lips together, plainly struggling not to laugh.

Mercifully, she says nothing more.

"Come on. We're here."

## Node: a_practical_arrangement

**Title:** A Perfectly Practical Arrangement  
**Image:** `emma_falkenheim_farewell.jpg` large  
**Emotion:** happy

**On enter:**

- Add story tag `emma_trusted_companion`.
- Add to Emma's known traits: **A companion beyond competition:** Emma enjoys Antonio's company even when there is no match to win or work to finish. Beneath her ambitious, demanding manner, she has begun to make room for companionship in her life.

By the time you've carried the trophy into the Academy's entrance hall, the night is well advanced. You set it down beneath the chandeliers, where it looks almost respectable again.

"Well, Fibonacci," Emma says, surveying it with satisfaction. "That's one thing successfully accomplished."

"Yes. I think we've earned ourselves a little rest."

Emma gives you a questioning look, as though the word _rest_ belongs to some obscure foreign language.

But then her expression changes.

"Listen, Antonio. I wanted to..."

She pauses, her gaze drifting toward the trophy, where both your names are engraved.

"I wanted to thank you. I don't know if I could have won without you."

There's an unfamiliar softness in her eyes. For an instant, you wonder if they might even be a little damp.

No. Not Emma. Must be the candlelight.

"You would have found a way," you tell her. "You always do."

"Perhaps." She smiles faintly. "But I'm rather glad I didn't have to."

She looks back at the engraved names.

"My parents gave everything they could to help me enter that tournament," she says quietly. "So I rather had to win."

For a moment, she says nothing more. You can almost see the familiar calculations turning behind her eyes: expenses, expectations, obligations, all the things she never quite manages to put aside.

Then she takes a breath, and something of her usual energy returns.

"You know what, Antonio? We make far too good a team to leave it at that. How about a regular study evening? My place, every Tuesday?"

She tilts her head, already adopting the air of someone presenting an entirely sensible academic proposal.

"It would be mutually beneficial, obviously. We could review our crafting techniques, compare strategies, perhaps work on a few new cards. There's no reason to let such a productive partnership go to waste."

Her eyes are smiling.

But there's a trace of nervousness there, too.

"And Antonio?"

"Yes?"

"Don't be late."

You watch her disappear up the stairs, looking rather pleased with herself.

For once, you suspect it has nothing to do with the trophy.
