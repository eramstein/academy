# Event: An Unnecessary Distraction

**Event ID:** `emma_unnecessary_distraction`

**Image:** Medium. Emma working on cards at her classroom desk, looking up with a slightly amused, questioning expression.

**Trigger:**

- Activity type: `class`
- Number of hangouts with Emma ≥ 2
- Emma is present
- Event has not occurred before

## Text

You arrive at the classroom a little earlier than usual, expecting to enjoy a few minutes of peace before Professor Glützenbaum begins his daily assault on the mysteries of artificery.

Unfortunately, someone has beaten you to it.

Emma is sitting at her usual desk, surrounded by a carefully organized assortment of cards, notes, and small jars of magical ingredients. She's examining a half-finished enchantment with the intense concentration of someone attempting to extract a confession from a particularly stubborn criminal.

"Good morning, Emma."

"Morning, Antonio."

She doesn't look up.

"Working on something interesting?"

"Very."

A few seconds pass.

"Would you care to elaborate?"

"Not especially."

Well. A promising start.

You pull up a chair beside her. Emma finally raises her eyes, giving you a look that suggests she's noticed your arrival approximately three minutes ago and has been patiently waiting for you to take the hint.

"Antonio, much as I appreciate your boundless curiosity, I'm trying to finish this before class."

"Perhaps I could help?"

"How thoughtful. Can you make the next fifteen minutes last half an hour?"

You consider the question. Time manipulation is rather beyond your current abilities.

"Then I'm afraid you'll have to settle for admiring my work in silence."

She returns to her cards, though the faint smile at the corner of her mouth suggests she isn't quite as annoyed as she pretends.

{{smittenThought}}

There must be _something_ you can do to justify staying a little longer.

## Conditional passages

### smittenThought

**When:** Emma has relation tag `EmmaSmitten`.

You could, of course, simply leave her alone. That would be the sensible thing to do. Unfortunately, your judgment becomes somewhat unreliable whenever Emma is within conversational distance. A deeply concerning condition, particularly for someone studying a discipline that demands precision and rational thought.

**Otherwise:**

You could leave her to her work. On the other hand, you've rather enjoyed your recent conversations with Emma, even if a surprising number of them involve her pointing out your shortcomings. Surely you can invent a legitimate reason to stay.

## Effects

- At the conclusion of any option, Emma gifts Antonio **one card chosen from three offered cards**, drawn from the pools `emma` and `base`.
- The narrative reason for the gift depends on the selected option.

## Options

### Option A: "Here, perhaps this will help."

**Requirement:** Antonio has at least 1 `magic_dust`.

**Text:**

You notice that the little jar of magic dust beside Emma is almost empty.

"Running short?"

"A little. I'll fetch more after class."

You reach into your bag and produce a small pouch of your own.

"Here. Take some of mine."

Emma looks up, surprised.

"Antonio, you don't have to—"

"I know. I've got enough for what I'm working on."

She hesitates, then accepts the pouch.

"Thank you. That's genuinely helpful."

She tips a little dust into the enchantment and carefully completes the pattern. A faint shimmer runs across the card before settling into a stable glow.

"There. Much better."

She examines the result with evident satisfaction, then turns to you.

"You know, I was planning to give this one a proper trial, but I already have several similar cards. Perhaps you'd make better use of it."

She lays three cards before you.

"Choose one. Consider it a fair exchange."

"You don't need to give me anything."

"Of course I don't. But I prefer to repay my debts before my creditors start charging interest."

She smiles warmly.

"And besides, I'd rather you put that card to good use. It'll make defeating you all the more satisfying."

**Outcomes:**

- Spend 1 `magic_dust`.
- Emma friendship +1.
- Antonio chooses and receives one card from three choices in pools `emma`, `base`.

### Option B: "I think I see a way to improve that."

**Check:** Intelligence — Medium.

**On success:**

**Text:**

You lean forward to examine the unfinished enchantment.

"You're channeling the secondary effect through the same binding as the first."

"Yes. That is generally how bindings work."

"Except the second effect doesn't need the full channel. Couldn't you split it here and use the smaller circuit?"

Emma opens her mouth to reply, then stops.

She looks down at the card.

"Hmm."

A rather encouraging sound.

She takes a quill and sketches a modification. You watch as her expression shifts from skepticism to genuine interest.

"That would reduce the strain on the primary enchantment..." she murmurs. "And leave enough capacity for an additional effect."

She considers the drawing for another moment, then looks at you.

"That's actually quite clever, Antonio."

You wait for the inevitable qualification.

None comes.

Emma seems to notice your expression.

"What?"

"Nothing. I'm just enjoying the moment."

"Good heavens. Is this the first time anyone has told you that you've had a good idea?"

"First time you've done it without immediately explaining why yours was better."

"Well, don't become accustomed to it."

She laughs and reaches for a small collection of finished cards.

"Here. You've earned something for your contribution."

She spreads three cards on the desk.

"Choose one. And thank you, Antonio. I might have wasted the entire morning on that binding."

"You're welcome."

"Now, if you don't mind, I'd like to finish it before the professor arrives."

A pause.

"Using _our_ solution, apparently."

**Outcomes on success:**

- Emma respect +1.
- Antonio chooses and receives one card from three choices in pools `emma`, `base`.

**On failure:**

**Text:**

You study the card for a moment.

"Wouldn't it work better if you moved that binding to the other side?"

Emma glances at the pattern.

"No."

"Are you sure?"

"Quite."

"Because it seems to me—"

"Antonio." She turns the card toward you and points to a series of tiny symbols. "That binding is stabilizing the entire enchantment. Moving it would cause the secondary effect to discharge into the primary circuit."

"Which would be bad?"

"Only if you consider setting fire to the desk a bad thing."

You look at the symbols again.

Ah. Yes. There they are.

"I was testing you."

"Naturally."

Emma gives you a look of exaggerated admiration.

"And I passed, I hope?"

"With distinction."

She smiles and returns to her work. A moment later, she seems to reconsider something.

"Although I suppose I should encourage such enthusiasm."

She selects three cards from her collection and lays them on the table.

"Here. Choose one. Perhaps studying a properly constructed enchantment will help you recognize one next time."

"A teaching aid?"

"Precisely. I consider it an investment in the quality of my future opponents."

**Outcomes on failure:**

- No relationship change.
- Antonio chooses and receives one card from three choices in pools `emma`, `base`.

### Option C: "Surely you can do better than that."

**Check:** Intelligence — Medium.

**On success:**

**Text:**

You study Emma's unfinished card with an expression of profound disappointment.

"Hmm. I expected better."

Her quill stops moving.

"Did you?"

"From the Academy's most promising student? Certainly."

Emma slowly turns toward you.

"Do enlighten me, Antonio. I'm always eager to learn from the great masters."

You point to a small section of the enchantment.

"Well, for a start, that secondary binding is terribly inefficient. Unless, of course, you're planning to power it with an entire sack of magic dust."

Emma looks at the card. Then at you.

Then back at the card.

"That," she says carefully, "is an interesting observation."

"Thank you. I do try."

"And entirely correct, unfortunately."

She picks up her quill and begins modifying the pattern.

You lean back, enjoying a rare and beautiful victory.

"I suppose even the great Emma Delamain has room for improvement."

"Naturally. Unlike you, I have never claimed to be infallible."

"Neither have I!"

"No, you merely behave as though the possibility of being wrong has never occurred to you."

She finishes the correction, inspects the card, and gives a satisfied nod.

"Very well, Fibonacci. You've caught me out."

She reaches into her collection and produces three cards.

"Choose one."

"What's this?"

"A reward for your extraordinary powers of observation."

"How generous."

"Don't flatter yourself. I'm giving you better cards because I'm tired of hearing excuses when I beat you."

You grin.

"Afraid I'll win if I have a decent deck?"

Emma's smile becomes positively dangerous.

"Thursday evenings are going to be very entertaining, aren't they?"

**Outcomes on success:**

- Emma respect +1.
- Antonio chooses and receives one card from three choices in pools `emma`, `base`.

**On failure:**

**Text:**

You lean over Emma's work and shake your head gravely.

"Oh dear."

She doesn't look up.

"Something wrong, Antonio?"

"I'm afraid so. That enchantment is rather... elementary, wouldn't you say?"

"Elementary?"

"Yes. I would have expected something more sophisticated from you."

Emma sets down her quill.

"How interesting. What would you suggest?"

You examine the card, searching desperately for some flaw that might justify your opening remark.

"Well, perhaps... the binding here could be improved?"

"How?"

"By changing its configuration."

"To what?"

An excellent question. One you had rather hoped she wouldn't ask.

Emma folds her hands and waits.

You begin to suspect you've made a tactical error.

"Actually, on closer inspection, it's quite good."

"How reassuring."

Her expression is perfectly composed, but her eyes are sparkling with amusement.

"Antonio, if you intend to criticize my work, I expect you to come armed with something more substantial than an impressive frown."

"Constructive criticism is an art."

"Indeed. And I look forward to the day you master it."

She returns to her cards, then pauses.

"Come to think of it, perhaps I can help you."

She selects three finished cards and lays them before you.

"Choose one."

"You're giving me a card?"

"Consider it remedial education. Study the enchantments carefully. Perhaps next time you'll be able to identify a genuine flaw."

"That's very kind of you."

"Oh, don't thank me. I have a selfish motive."

"Which is?"

She smiles sweetly.

"I'd hate for our rivalry to become one-sided."

**Outcomes on failure:**

- No relationship change.
- Antonio chooses and receives one card from three choices in pools `emma`, `base`.

## Closing note

The professor enters the classroom just as you finish examining your new acquisition.

Emma gathers her notes and gives you a sidelong glance.

"Well, Antonio, that was certainly an interesting interruption."

"You're welcome."

"I didn't say it was a _useful_ interruption."

She pauses, then allows herself a small smile.

"Though I suppose it wasn't entirely unwelcome."

She turns toward the lectern, leaving you with a new card and the distinct impression that your morning has been rather more successful than you had any right to expect.
