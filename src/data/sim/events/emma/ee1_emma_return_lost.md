## Node 1 — The Long Road Home

The carriage rolls out of Falkenheim beneath a grey afternoon sky.

At least the organizers have had the decency to provide transportation home. You and Emma sit opposite each other on comfortable upholstered benches, surrounded by a silence considerably less comfortable than the seats.

Emma has been staring out of the window for the better part of twenty minutes.

"Well," she says at last. "That was humiliating."

You consider pointing out that you made it remarkably far in a tournament filled with some of the Academy's finest players. Unfortunately, Emma has already begun a detailed reconstruction of your defeat.

"If I'd played the third turn differently... No, that wouldn't have worked either. Unless we'd changed the opening sequence. Or perhaps—"

"Emma."

"—if we'd anticipated their counterspell, we could have—"

"Emma."

She turns toward you.

"What?"

"The tournament's over."

She gives you a look that suggests this is an exceptionally unhelpful observation.

"Yes, Fibonacci. I had noticed."

"And we lost."

"Thank you. Do go on. Your analysis is fascinating."

"And we're still here."

Emma opens her mouth, then closes it.

You gesture vaguely between the two of you.

"We're both alive. We learned a few things. We even made some rather good cards. All things considered, I think we could have done worse."

She looks at you for a moment.

"That is a remarkably generous interpretation of events."

"One of my many talents."

"Alongside losing tournaments?"

"Especially that one."

Despite herself, she lets out a short laugh.

It's not much, but it's the first time she's smiled since the final match.

## Node: the_carriage_ride

**Title:** A Quiet Journey  
**Image:** `emma_falkenheim_carriage_evening.jpg` large  
**Emotion:** neutral

The carriage follows the winding road through fields and wooded hills. Little by little, the disappointment of the tournament begins to loosen its grip.

Emma eventually puts away her notes. This alone feels like a significant achievement.

For a while, you talk about the matches, the opponents, and the rather extraordinary things people are willing to do in pursuit of a trophy. Then the conversation wanders elsewhere, and finally fades into a comfortable silence.

Emma takes a book from her bag and begins reading.

Every now and then, she shares a passage she finds interesting, followed by an explanation that assumes you possess approximately twice your actual knowledge of the subject.

"You really ought to read more, Antonio."

"You really ought to recommend shorter books."

"Philistine."

But she is smiling.

As the afternoon fades, exhaustion catches up with you. You close your eyes for what you intend to be a moment.

When you wake, the carriage is dark except for the lantern swaying above the driver's seat.

A blanket has been draped over you.

Emma is still reading, though her book rests lower in her lap now. She notices you're awake and closes it.

"Feeling better?"

"A little. How long was I asleep?"

"Long enough for me to enjoy several hours of uninterrupted intellectual peace."

"You're welcome."

She gives you a small smile.

"Actually, it was rather nice."

You aren't entirely sure whether she means the peace or the company.

Perhaps both.

The carriage slows as the Academy's lights appear ahead.

"Well," Emma says, gathering her things. "Home again."

## Node: a_practical_arrangement

**Title:** A Perfectly Practical Arrangement  
**Image:** `emma_falkenheim_farewell.jpg` large  
**Emotion:** happy

**On enter:**

- Add story tag `emma_trusted_companion`.
- Add to Emma's known traits: **A companion beyond competition:** Emma enjoys Antonio's company even when there is no match to win or work to finish. She has begun to value their partnership independently of its results.

The Academy's entrance hall is almost deserted at this hour. Your footsteps echo across the marble floor as you and Emma make your way toward the stairs.

"Well, Fibonacci," she says. "Not quite the glorious return we'd imagined."

"No. But at least we didn't have to walk."

"A modest consolation."

She pauses at the foot of the stairs.

"Listen, Antonio. I wanted to say something."

Her sudden seriousness catches you off guard.

"I know how much work you put into this tournament. And I know I wasn't always the easiest person to work with."

"Really? I hadn't noticed."

"Don't push your luck."

She smiles, then looks down for a moment.

"My parents made considerable sacrifices so I could enter. I wanted so badly to make it worthwhile."

For once, she doesn't try to hide the disappointment in her voice.

"I'm sorry we couldn't win," you say.

"So am I."

She takes a breath.

"But you know what? I don't regret going. Not even a little."

She looks up at you.

"We made a rather good team, didn't we?"

"I thought so."

"And it would be a terrible waste to stop now."

Her expression brightens as an idea takes shape.

"How about a regular study evening? My place, every Tuesday?"

She adopts her most businesslike tone.

"We could refine our crafting techniques, work on new cards, review our mistakes. An entirely sensible arrangement. Mutually beneficial, obviously."

She smiles.

But beneath that confident little speech, you notice a trace of nervousness.

## Options

### Option A: Accept warmly

**Text:**

"I'd like that, Emma."

For an instant, her face lights up with unmistakable delight.

Then she straightens, all business again.

"Excellent. It's a deal, Fibonacci. Tuesdays, seven o'clock. Bring your books. You already know where I live."

She turns to leave, then looks back over her shoulder.

"And Antonio?"

"Yes?"

"Next time, we're winning."

You grin.

"Naturally."

She starts toward the stairs, then pauses.

"And Antonio? I did enjoy working with you."

Before you can answer, she adds, "Despite the considerable disadvantages."

There she is.

"Thank you, Emma. I'd like to, but I don't think I can promise every Tuesday."

"Oh."

The disappointment crosses her face before she can conceal it.

"Of course. I understand. We both have rather a lot on our plates."

She adjusts the strap of her bag, suddenly very interested in making sure it sits properly on her shoulder.

"Well. The offer stands, should your schedule become less troublesome."

"Thanks, Emma."

She looks at you again, her expression softening.

"And Antonio? I meant what I said. About the tournament."

She pauses.

"I couldn't have asked for a better partner."

With a final smile, she heads toward the stairs.
