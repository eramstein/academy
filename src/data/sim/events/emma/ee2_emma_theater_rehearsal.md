Event 2: Neverland, Briefly

NPC: emma
Trigger: Scheduled event;
Emotion: amused

Text

You arrive at the theater prepared for an afternoon of dignified artistic collaboration. This confidence lasts until Emma steps out of the side entrance.

Her costume is a delicate confection of green silk, embroidered leaves, and translucent wings that catch the afternoon light. Someone has arranged her hair with tiny silver ornaments. The effect is devastating.

You have seen Emma in an elegant dress, in Academy uniform, and in the middle of an argument with Elsa. None of these experiences has prepared you for this.

She notices your expression instantly. Naturally.

"Good afternoon, Peter." A smile threatens the corners of her mouth. "Is something wrong?"

"I didn't know they made fairies in that shape."

"What shape did you expect? A turnip with wings?"

You attempt to recover the dignity you have so carelessly misplaced.

Emma gives a little turn, setting the wings shimmering, and watches you with undisguised amusement.

"Before you get your hopes up, Fibonacci, I have read the script. Our relationship is strictly platonic. Rather disappointingly, I spend most of it being jealous of Wendy and nearly dying."

"Nearly?"

"Don't look so hopeful. I survive."

Title: Second Star to the Left, Then Straight Into Disaster
Emotion: exasperated
Image: larg

Text

The rehearsal begins with Professor Lombard declaring that the theater is a place of discipline and mutual respect.

This principle survives for approximately forty seconds.

The Lost Boys have learned three different versions of their opening lines, none of which match the script. Two students argue over who is supposed to play Wendy. Farid refuses to wear his hook because it is "an insult to functional craftsmanship," then replaces it with an actual metal attachment of his own design.

"Farid," Lombard says, "why is your hook smoking?"

"It's an improvement."

Molly, who has taken her role with a commitment bordering on religious devotion, crawls out from behind the scenery and bellows a crocodilian roar so convincing that a Lost Boy abandons the stage through a window.

"Molly!" calls Lombard. "The crocodile is meant to be silent!"

"But Professor, it has a clock inside!"

"Then tick!"

When your turn comes to fly, the stage harness lifts you precisely two feet and deposits you into a painted cardboard tree. Emma, waiting in the wings, makes a sound suspiciously like a suppressed laugh.

"Don't just stand there, Tinker Bell," you protest. "Help me!"

"I'm six inches tall, remember? What exactly do you propose?"

The tree falls over. Its trunk strikes the pirate ship, whose mast snaps, bringing down a painted moon. Farid ducks magnificently. Molly tries to catch it with her mouth.

There is a tremendous crash.

In the settling dust, Professor Lombard stands motionless beside the remains of Neverland.

"Right," she says at last. "The production is cancelled."

No one argues.

"Mr. Fibonacci. Miss Delamain. You were the last two to arrive. Kindly help restore the theater before you leave. The rest of you: out."

The cast departs at a speed that suggests the Academy should consider competitive athletics as an alternative to drama.

When the doors finally close, you and Emma survey the wreckage.

"Well," she says. "I thought your flight was moving."

"I was aiming for the stars."

"You reached a shrub. A respectable first attempt."

Soon you are dragging scenery upright, retrieving scattered props, and trying not to laugh every time you discover another casualty of the performance. Emma puts on Farid's abandoned pirate hat and delivers a remarkably fierce impression of him. You respond with your best Professor Lombard, waving a broken wand and declaring the entire building a disciplinary offense.

At one point, a painted star gets tangled in Emma's wings. You carefully free it while she holds very still. For a brief moment, you are standing rather close together.

"Thank you, Peter," she says, softly.

Then she plucks the star from your hair. "You seem to have brought half of Neverland with you."

By the time the stage is clear, the light outside has begun to fade. Emma kneels to retrieve some old papers from beneath a loose floorboard.

"Antonio? Look at this."

Among yellowed playbills lies an aged parchment covered in intricate diagrams and a distinctive Italian script. Emma studies it, and her amusement gives way to astonishment.

"This isn't a theatrical prop. It's an artifact pattern—an Italian Codex!"

She spreads it out on a crate. "A real one. It allows its owner to craft Italian cards. You know they're my favorites. The compositions, the elegance, the sheer audacity of some of their effects..."

For several seconds she is utterly absorbed. Then she rolls up the parchment and offers it to you.

"Here. You should have it."

"Emma, you just said—"

"I know what I said. But artifact crafting and I have a rather strained relationship. Give me a deck to build and I'll make it sing. Give me a workshop and I'm liable to produce an expensive paperweight." She smiles ruefully. "You, on the other hand, could actually make this thing."

She holds out the pattern, waiting for your answer.

Emma looks at the parchment, then at you. Her usual quick retort does not come.

"I do my best."

"Yes. That's rather the problem."

She tucks the parchment carefully into her satchel. "Thank you, Antonio. Really."

As you leave, she bumps your shoulder with hers. "If I ever manage to craft it, you'll be the first to know. After I finish celebrating, naturally."
