## Text

Tuesday evening. Your regular study session with Emma, an arrangement that has become something of a tradition. Tonight, however, there's an additional incentive.

Earlier that afternoon, Emma challenged you to solve a particularly difficult enchantment problem before she does. If you succeed, she'll give you one of her latest creations: an exceptionally fine blue card you've had your eye on for some time.

"And what happens if I win?" you asked.

"Well, Antonio, I thought it would be rather unkind to demand a prize from someone who has just suffered such a humiliating defeat."

The nerve of that woman.

You're still contemplating the many ways in which you intend to prove her wrong when you knock on her door.

Emma opens with her usual friendly smile. As usual, she's wearing a comfortable shirt. And, as usual, she's... oh.

Not quite as usual.

She's wearing shorts. Perfectly ordinary, comfortable indoor shorts. Admittedly, they appear to have been designed with rather extreme summer temperatures in mind.

{{smitten_reaction}}

"Antonio! Come in. I've prepared everything." She steps aside with an innocent smile. "I do hope you've brought your best ideas. You'll need them."

A quarter of an hour later, things are not going well.

Emma has made considerably more progress than you. She's settled sideways into her armchair, one foot resting on an armrest, her long legs crossed comfortably in front of her. Occasionally she stretches, shifts position, or leans over to consult a reference book, apparently without a care in the world.

You, meanwhile, have been staring at the same enchantment diagram for several minutes.

There is a perfectly respectable mathematical explanation for why the third resonance coefficient refuses to balance.

You would very much like to remember what it is.

Across the room, Emma makes another annotation. Then she glances at you over the top of her book.

"Everything all right, Fibonacci? You've been awfully quiet."

There's something suspiciously satisfied about that smile.

You return your attention to the diagram.

The resonance coefficient. The secondary binding. The asymmetrical distribution of magical energy.

Of course.

Emma looks up, still wearing that infuriatingly satisfied little smile.

"Oh, very well! You've earned your prize, you insufferable man."

The shirt, however, is another matter.

It's a light, delicate linen garment, the sort one might wear on the hottest summer afternoon. The fabric is just translucent enough to make you suspect that Emma has interpreted your concerns with extraordinary selectivity.

She looks up, her cheeks faintly flushed, evidently struggling to keep a straight face.

"There. Is that better?"

She smooths the fabric over one knee with an air of complete innocence.

"I wouldn't want your academic performance to suffer on my account."

You look at her.

She looks at you.

This woman is enjoying herself far too much.

**Text:**

"You know what, Emma? You're right."

"Am I?"

"It's terribly warm in here."

You reach for the top button of your shirt.

Emma's smile falters.

"Antonio..."

You undo the button.

"I really ought to make myself more comfortable."

A second button.

"After all, as you so wisely pointed out, we're among friends."

Emma sits up abruptly.

"Fibonacci!"

You reach for a third.

She snatches a folded blanket from the arm of her chair and pulls it up in front of herself like a defensive rampart.

"All right! All right! That's quite enough!"

You stop, looking at her with exaggerated innocence.

"Something wrong with my clothes, Emma?"

For a moment, she glares at you over the blanket.

Then the absurdity of the entire situation catches up with her.

She starts laughing.

You follow almost immediately.

Within seconds, neither of you can maintain the slightest pretense of dignity. Emma collapses back into her chair, laughing so hard she has to wipe her eyes.

"Oh, Antonio! You wretched man!"

"Merely adapting to the climate."

"Of course you are!"

Eventually, she manages to recover enough to throw the blanket aside and retrieve her notes.

"Right. A truce. We have an enchantment to finish."

"And the competition?"

"Oh, forget the competition. At this rate, neither of us will graduate."

You settle beside her, still exchanging occasional smiles, and return to the problem. Without the ridiculous contest of distractions, the solution comes surprisingly quickly. Together, you work out an elegant way to stabilize the enchantment.

As you gather your notes, Emma gives you a warm, lingering smile.

"That was a rather enjoyable evening, Antonio."

"Despite my deplorable academic performance?"

"Entirely because of it, I suspect."

She pauses.

"And for the record, you looked perfectly respectable before you started undressing."
