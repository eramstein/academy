Text
The administrator finishes counting your tuition money, places it carefully in a drawer, and stamps your enrollment papers with an air of solemn finality.

"Welcome to the Academy, Mister Fibonacci. Your academic career officially begins today."

Wonderful. Three years of savings have just become a piece of paper with a stamp on it. You sincerely hope the exchange proves worthwhile.

"Now, I imagine you'll need some assistance finding your bearings," the administrator continues. "Classrooms, schedules, accommodations, that sort of thing. I suggest you speak to the Dude. He's just outside my office."

You blink.

"The... Dude?"

"Yes, the Dude. He'll show you around."

There is something deeply unsettling about hearing the word dude emerge from a man who has just insisted that you sign your name in three different places using your full legal title.

Nevertheless, you thank him and step outside.

Leaning against the wall beside the office is a man who looks to be in his forties. His clothes are comfortable rather than fashionable, his hair rather longer than Academy regulations probably recommend, and he is contemplating a small crack in the ceiling with the serene concentration of a philosopher examining the mysteries of existence.

He notices you looking at him.

"Hey, man."

You hesitate. "Are you... the Dude?"

"That's what they call me."

"What's your actual name?"

He considers the question for a moment.

"Well, that's a pretty complicated question, man. Depends who's asking."

"Antonio Fibonacci. First-year student."

"Ah, Antonio. Nice. Welcome to the great institution of learning, my friend." He gestures vaguely at the building. "Lot of learning going on around here. Lot of people taking it very seriously, too."

He doesn't sound entirely convinced this is a good thing.

"So, you work here?"

"Nah, man. I'm a student."

You look at him again.

"A student?"

"First year."

You look at him again.

The Dude smiles, apparently quite accustomed to this reaction.

"Yeah, I know. Been a first-year for... twenty years? Twenty-one? Time's a funny thing, man."

"Twenty years?!"

"Something like that. Never really kept count." He shrugs. "They keep changing the curriculum anyway. Every time I think I've got it figured out, someone invents a new examination."

You struggle to imagine how a man could spend two decades at the Academy without graduating.

Or being expelled.

Or, for that matter, becoming a professor.

"Doesn't the administration mind?"

"Oh, sure. Some of them get pretty worked up about it." He waves a hand toward the office. "But we have an understanding. I attend enough classes, pass a few things, everybody stays reasonably happy. It's a delicate balance, man. You don't want to disturb it."

You begin to suspect that the Academy's administrative regulations contain loopholes of truly impressive dimensions.

"Anyway," he continues, pushing himself away from the wall, "I got a little gathering planned for tonight. Cafeteria. All the new first-years. Figured you folks should get to know each other before the professors start filling your heads with complicated stuff."

He grins.

"Nothing fancy. Just hanging out, talking, making friends. Maybe preventing a few lifelong academic rivalries before they get started. Though those can be pretty entertaining, too."

He gives you an encouraging pat on the shoulder.

"So what do you say, Antonio? You coming?"

"See, that's the spirit. Academy's a big place. Easy to get lost in all the rules and books and competitive nonsense. But you find yourself a few good people, share a meal, have a laugh..."

He spreads his hands.

"That's the important stuff."

You nod. After spending three years working to afford your tuition, making a few friends sounds like a pleasant change of pace.

"Tonight, then. Cafeteria?"

"That's the place, man. Evening. Don't worry too much about being on time. Time's more of a suggestion anyway."

He gives you a casual wave and resumes his inspection of the ceiling.

You leave him to it.

Perhaps there's something interesting up there.

Or perhaps you've just met the least ambitious man in the Academy.

Either way, you suspect he might be worth knowing.
