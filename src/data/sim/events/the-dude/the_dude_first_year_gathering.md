Text
The Academy cafeteria is considerably quieter in the evening. Most students have finished their meals, leaving behind a few scattered groups, the occasional clatter of dishes, and the comforting smell of something that might once have been a stew.

You spot the Dude immediately. He's lounging at the head of a long table, looking as though he's been sitting there since the Academy was founded. A collection of young men and women have gathered around him, engaged in various degrees of conversation.

"Antonio! My man!" He waves you over with a broad smile. "Glad you made it. Grab a seat, grab a drink. We're just getting started."

You sit down, attracting several curious glances.

"All right, everybody," the Dude announces, making a vague sweeping gesture. "This here is Antonio. Good guy. Just arrived today. Antonio, these are your fellow travelers on the great journey of academic enlightenment."

He pauses, apparently satisfied with this description.

"Or something like that. Anyway, introductions."

He points toward a young woman sitting with impeccable posture at the far end of the table. Her clothes are elegant, her expression composed, and her gaze suggests she has already assessed the social standing of everyone present.

"That's Elsa."

"Elsa von..." she begins, but the Dude has already moved on.

"Good to meet you, Elsa," he adds.

She closes her mouth. The look she gives him could probably freeze a small lake.

"Charmed," she says to you, in a tone that implies she has yet to reach a final decision on the matter.

Next is a dark-haired young man sitting somewhat apart from the others, listening more than speaking.

"Farid," the Dude says. "Quiet fellow. Seems all right."

Farid inclines his head politely. "Good evening, Antonio."

He offers nothing further, but his expression is friendly enough. You get the impression he's the sort of person who prefers to observe before deciding whether a conversation is worth joining.

"And that's Vaiana," the Dude continues, gesturing toward another young woman.

"Hello, Antonio!" she says, giving you a cheerful wave. "Welcome to the madness."

"See?" the Dude remarks. "Already getting along. Beautiful."

The Dude surveys the table with an expression of profound satisfaction.

"Look at that. Everybody getting acquainted. Civilization, man. That's how it starts."

"Actually," Elsa interrupts, "civilization generally begins with the establishment of laws and institutions."

"Sure, sure," the Dude agrees amiably. "Those too."

He takes a leisurely sip from his cup.

"Oh, right. Almost forgot. There's supposed to be another guy here. Ousmane. Had something else going on tonight, apparently. Very busy fellow."

He shrugs.

"Can't be everywhere at once."

"An astonishing observation," Elsa murmurs.

"Thanks, man."

You suspect Elsa is discovering, to her considerable frustration, that sarcasm is not particularly effective against someone who refuses to recognize it.

There is one student the Dude hasn't introduced yet. A young man with untidy red hair and a gentle, somewhat nervous expression sits near the end of the table, quietly turning his cup between his hands. He has spent most of the evening listening, occasionally smiling at something funny but rarely speaking himself.

You catch his eye, and he gives you an awkward little smile.

"Oh! Hello. I'm Henry."

"Antonio. Nice to meet you."

"Likewise." He hesitates, then adds, "I think we might be roommates, actually."

That gets your attention.

"Roommates?"

"In the barracks. I saw the accommodation list earlier. Antonio Fibonacci, wasn't it? We're assigned to the same room."

"Ah, excellent!" the Dude exclaims. "Roommates! Look at that. Already making connections."

Henry smiles apologetically.

"I hope you don't mind. I'm fairly quiet, generally. And I don't have much luggage, so I shouldn't take up too much space."

He looks genuinely concerned that his very existence might inconvenience you.

You assure him that neither of those things is a problem.

"That's good," he says, visibly relaxing. "I've never shared a room before. I was a little nervous about it."

"Well, you're in luck," the Dude declares. "Antonio's a solid guy. I can tell."

"You've known him for about five minutes," Elsa observes.

"Exactly. First impressions, man. Very important."

Henry chuckles softly.

The conversation eventually turns to accommodations. You learn that Henry is the only other person at the table staying in the Academy barracks. Everyone else has found lodgings at one of the inns in town, ranging from modest establishments to considerably more prestigious addresses.

"One does appreciate a certain standard of living," Elsa remarks, smoothing an imaginary crease from her sleeve.

"Sure," the Dude agrees. "Comfort's important. Me, I find you can get comfortable pretty much anywhere if you give it enough time."

"Some of us," Elsa replies, "have rather more exacting standards."

"Hey, whatever works for you."

Once again, Elsa finds herself without a satisfactory target.

As the evening progresses, the conversation becomes livelier. Students compare their expectations, their fears, and the alarming number of subjects listed on the first-year curriculum. Even Farid contributes the occasional dry observation, while Henry gradually grows comfortable enough to join in without being directly addressed.

The Dude mostly listens, interjecting now and then with observations of questionable relevance and surprising wisdom.

Eventually, he raises his cup.

"All right, folks. Here's to the first year."

"Your twenty-first?" someone asks.

"Could be, man. Could be."

He smiles serenely.

"Here's hoping it's a good one."

Cups are raised around the table. Even Elsa participates, although she looks faintly embarrassed by the informality of the occasion.

And there you are, surrounded by a collection of strangers who will, for better or worse, be sharing the next chapter of your life.

A proud young woman with an aristocratic disdain for almost everything. A quiet, observant young man. A gentle redhead who will apparently be sleeping a few feet away from you. And several others whose personalities you're only beginning to discover.

Presiding over the gathering is a forty-one-year-old first-year student who seems to have achieved a kind of enlightenment through the simple expedient of never graduating.

You came to the Academy to learn artificery, master the game of Hordes, and perhaps make something of yourself.

You have a feeling the education might extend rather further than you expected.
