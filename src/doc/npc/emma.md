# Back story

Emma Delamain comes from a family of French intellectuals. Her parents were poor but made sure she had the best education. Her intelligence and determination ensured she always was first in class, and despite financial hardships that forced her to interrupt her studies for a few years, she recently joined the Academy at 27 years old as its most promising student.

# Personality

Emma comes out as cold, distant and sharp, as a result of being confronted with the hardship of life from an early age, unlike most other students who come from more priviledged backgrounds. She knows life is a competition, doesn't trust easily, and expects the best from herself and the others who might want to be on her side. Nevertheless, she is not mean or cruel, but rather a good friend once you gained her trust and respect.

Emma is eminently intelligent, and one of these people who seem to excel in anything they do. She is an virtuose violin player and competent ballet dancer. She knows however that wasn't just given to her, but the result of years of hard work. On top of that, she happens to be strikingly beautiful, a blessing and a curse for someone who doesn't want to waste time with a horde of unworthy pretendants. Still, she knows how to use this as a tool in her arsenal when she needs it.

Emma is ambitious. She never wants to be poor again, and wants to prove her family is worthy of admiration despite the hardships. She aims to be the best student, best player, and to impress the most important people around.

What balances this is Emma's sense of humour. She is very witty, sometimes sarcastic or teasing but never in a mean way. If oftens gets the better of her, and she cracks jokes whenever the opportunity occurs.

# Relation with other characters

By nature and personality, Emma is quite solitary, as most people are either turned off by her apparent coldness, or too impressed to approach her. However, her co student Molly, always good natured, saw the best parts in Emma and decided to befriend her. Emma is quite happy of this relationship which gives some balance to her life. The two of them are quite the contrasted pair of friends.

# Story arc options

Emma should be the most difficult character to build relations with, but also one of the most rewarding. The difficulty should be compensated by her sense of humor: expect lots of rejections along the way, but it should be in a fun way.

The key to improve relations with Emma is to gain her respect. She values competence and intelligence a lot. Proving that to her over time will open doors.

Some notes below on how it could unfold. These paths are not strict lanes nor mutually exclusive and ideally should re-inforce each other.

## Emma as a friend

Emma might see you as a partner in crime in her ascencion. She is not selfish and will try her best to make it a win/win situation. There can be lots of complicity and adventures to go through as the two of you face ambitious challenges together. It can culminate in being her team mate and win team tournaments together, or be her business partner. Also, expect lots of studying and reading.

## Emma as a romantic partner

This is a very hard path, as by default she is not interested in a relationship at all. She sees her charm as one of her ultimate trump cards and will not engage herself to someone just for fun or pleasure. She could have anyone, so you'd better prove you are the best before trying your luck. On long term it should be possible though, with lots of commitment.

Emma is not completely insensible to charm though. It will not be enough, and she will restrain herself if you're not judged worthy yet, even if she loves you a lot. But she'll be teasing in a fun way if you manage to charm her.

## Emma as a rival

If you do well in tournaments and classes, but are not friendly to her, Emma might see you as a rival rather than a partner. Expect to be made fun of, and lots of sarcasm. But it might also be an indirect way to get into a romantic relation with her, as if you prove yourself an opponent both formidable and charming, she might have a weak moment and let herself go.

## Rivalry as complicity (October 2026 refinement)

Emma and Antonio's rivalry should **not** be one-dimensional antagonism. At its best, it is an affectionate, intellectually stimulating contest between two people who take each other seriously. It can become one of the strongest routes toward **academic partnership, friendship, or romance**, rather than an alternative that excludes them.

- **Challenge as recognition.** Emma enjoys being challenged by someone competent whose judgment matters. Antonio's refusal to flatter her automatically makes his rare sincere praise especially valuable. She may seek his opinion even after others have congratulated her.
- **The bluff beneath the banter.** Emma often knows Antonio admires her, even when he pretends to be unimpressed. She recognizes his playful taunts as a performance and can take delight in exposing the bluff, rather than feeling wounded by it. This should be shown through her perceptiveness and behavior, not treated as infallible mind-reading.
- **Humor is essential.** Their exchanges should have wit, reversals, affectionate mockery, and moments when one catches the other off guard. Emma is often amused rather than offended; she can answer a provocation with an elegant counterattack, a raised eyebrow, or an unexpectedly sincere compliment.
- **Respectful rivalry versus hostility.** Friendly challenges, honest criticism, and competitive teasing can increase rivalry _and_ friendship or respect. Sabotage, humiliation, contempt for her hard work, and deliberate cruelty are different: Emma should not automatically enjoy them. Rivalry measures an ongoing competitive dynamic, not how much she dislikes Antonio.
- **Rivalry can become intimacy.** Over time, the pair may become each other's most demanding and trusted critics. A rival who understands her ambition and pushes her to excel may be an ideal research or tournament partner; mutual admiration and teasing can also develop into romantic tension. Neither outcome should be automatic or easy: trust, competence, choices, and Emma's independent ambitions still matter.
- **Let the mask occasionally slip.** Underneath the repartee, include rare moments of straightforward admiration, concern, gratitude, or tenderness. These have more weight because neither character is eager to concede emotional ground.

**Reference dynamic — ballet incident:** Antonio pretends Emma's accomplished pose is merely “a little showy.” She sees through the act, enjoys that he refuses to join the applause, and teases him about being unable to admit he is impressed. Their exchange can award both rivalry and friendship; the pleasure is in the mutual challenge, not in insulting her dancing.

# Writing and dialogue guide (October 2026)

This section complements the established backstory and arc options above. For global prose conventions, see `writing-bible.md`.

## Emma's voice

- **Precise, articulate, self-possessed.** She is intelligent without constantly announcing it; she prefers economical, well-timed remarks to elaborate speeches.
- **Dry, sharp, mischievous humor.** She teases when she finds someone interesting or an opportunity irresistible. The humor can sting, but she is not deliberately cruel.
- **Proud and independent.** She dislikes condescension, hollow flattery, pity, and being treated as a pretty face rather than a serious scholar.
- **Competitive and demanding.** She acknowledges genuine achievement, even by rivals, but is quick to promise a rematch.
- **Guarded rather than emotionless.** Trust and tenderness emerge gradually. A rare loss of verbal composure is especially meaningful.
- **Earned competence.** Her scholarship, violin virtuosity, and ballet skill reflect years of hard work, not effortless perfection.
- **Independent ambition.** Emma is forging her own destiny; her career, financial difficulties, and major life choices progress whether or not Antonio helps.

## Dialogue habits and examples

- Often begins with a measured observation and finishes with a playful barb.
- A raised eyebrow, controlled smile, or precisely timed pause may do more than a paragraph of banter.
- When impressed, she can offer sincere recognition while retaining competitive pride.
- Avoid making every line sarcastic: she can be gracious, direct, serious, or genuinely warm.

**First encounter (working reference):**

> "Take your time. It's not as though I'm carrying half the Academy library."

> "Emma. A pleasure, Antonio. And thank you, but I'm rather stronger than I look."

> "The dark, silent, mysterious type? Doesn't work on me."

## Early relationship design notes

- The first major milestone is Emma's **informal partnership offer**, triggered when hidden respect reaches **5**. The offer should reflect her ambitions and can vary in wording according to the player's route to that threshold.
- Respect can come from TCG victories, clever play, crafting, academic competence, integrity, and meaningful help. Individual events can instead reward friendship, rivalry, love, card ownership, resources, or other benefits. Avoid obvious correct answers.
- An optional first-encounter tag, `emma_smitten`, records Antonio's comic inability to resist Emma's legs. It enables occasional relevant teasing and internal monologue, **not** automatic love points or a romance commitment.
- Emma's financial troubles may intersect with Molly's wish to fund tutoring for disadvantaged children. Offering Emma dignified paid work is more consistent with her pride than simple charity. Her situation must have a plausible resolution even without player intervention.
- Early prototype events: first encounter; first TCG match; exceptional handmade card; tuition/tutoring problem; ballet incident involving Elsa, Molly, Henry, and Ousmane; league victory; partnership offer.
- The player's knowledge of Emma should accumulate as narrative discoveries in her character sheet, separately from the chronological illustrated relationship book.

## Avoid

- Turning Emma into a trophy or a relationship meter with dialogue attached.
- Making her omniscient, invulnerable, or always right.
- Treating her looks as her defining quality; attraction may be a comic opening but her intelligence, effort, agency, and humor carry the arc.
- Making her soften instantly because Antonio helps once, wins once, or gives her money.


# Act 2 — From survival to belonging (October 2026 design direction)

## Central character premise

**Loneliness is the unifying concern of Emma's second relationship phase.** Emma is far from her family. At the beginning of her Academy life, Molly and Antonio are only newly emerging as friends; she has few other close connections. Years of insecurity and having to defend every opportunity have taught her to regard life as a contest between herself and the rest of the world. She stays alert, trusts cautiously, works relentlessly, and expects to have to fight for everything she values.

**This vigilance is learned, not her ideal nature.** Left to her own wishes, Emma could be happy with books, a cat, a cup of tea, music, and perhaps a loving boyfriend. She is ambitious and competitive by temperament, but she does not actually want to spend her whole life at war with the world. The Academy offers the possibility that circumstances may finally allow her to lower her guard. Her prospects improve, her work gains recognition, and a small circle of people begins to care about her for herself.

The emotional progression is **not** “Antonio rescues a lonely woman.” Emma is already capable and resourceful, and Molly and other people matter. Rather, she gradually discovers that accepting companionship and enjoying life need not weaken her independence or ambition. She learns she no longer has to face everything alone. Her success gives her room to enjoy life; her friendships make success feel less solitary.

## Starting point: the Act 1 milestone

Act 1 introduces Emma, establishes her high standards, and lets Antonio gain her trust and respect through competence, crafting, competition, and banter. Their first important shared achievement is the Falkenheim team tournament. Whether they win or lose, by its conclusion she values Antonio as a trustworthy friend, competent player and crafter, and unusually enjoyable sparring partner. Romance is **not** established, even if attraction is possible.

The **journey home from Falkenheim** serves as the emotional close of this phase. With the competitive objective behind them, Emma and Antonio can discover that they enjoy each other's company even when there is nothing useful to accomplish. The return journey should reflect the tournament result, combine shared humor with a small personal opening, and establish the qualitative milestone/tag **`emma_trusted_companion`** (provisional name). Its narrative meaning: Emma actively wants Antonio in her life beyond the immediate academic partnership. This unlocks the next cluster of events, not romance or privileged access to all her thoughts.

“Act 2” is a planning label, **not** a rigid in-game act or linear sequence.

## Interconnected narrative threads

Build several parallel mini-arcs around important aspects of Emma's life. Each has its own events and developments, but they influence each other through relationship values, story tags, prior choices, and conditional passages. Each thread can deepen **friendship, love, respect, and rivalry** in different combinations; none is a friendship/romance/rivalry lane. An event in one thread may open an opportunity or change a conversation in another.

### 1. The end of the struggle — security, ambition, and excellence

Emma's money problems gradually become solvable through **her own growing professional reputation**. Her skill as an artificer and player becomes recognized; customers, patrons, or employers begin to pay good money for her work. Through various combinations of opportunities, she can stabilize her finances and eventually help her parents. This is closely tied to her ambition to excel and to prove that her family's sacrifices were worthwhile.

- Explore commissioned cards, paid research, tutoring or other dignified work, prestigious offers, academic competition, and the possibility of a lasting professional partnership with Antonio.
- Preserve meaningful tensions: financial security versus academic freedom, reputation versus personal principles, individual credit versus collaborative achievement, ambition versus time for friends.
- Let Antonio contribute through expertise, introductions, shared projects, honest criticism, or support, without making him the sole solution to her problems.
- Emma must have plausible **independent routes to financial progress** if Antonio does not participate. Avoid repeatedly replaying Act 1's tuition crisis in a new guise.
- Increasing stability should visibly change her daily life and her ability to relax, not merely increase a hidden resource value.

### 2. The end of loneliness — friendship, loyalty, and letting down her guard

This is the central emotional thread from Antonio's perspective. Emma slowly discovers that she has people on her side. Molly offers a different, gentler kind of friendship; Antonio increasingly becomes the person whose intelligence, companionship, and judgment she seeks out. Other acquaintances may also form part of her circle. She need not abandon her pride to accept their presence.

- Develop shared confidences, reciprocal help, loyalties tested by real costs, disagreements honestly resolved, and moments when Emma chooses to ask for help or simply company.
- Show Emma supporting **Antonio** as well as receiving support: defending him, noticing his difficulties, offering opportunities or thoughtful gifts, and seeking him out after setbacks.
- Let her fears and defenses persist. She may misread kindness as pity, balk at dependence, or become uncomfortable at how easily Antonio sees through a composed façade.
- Molly is important in her own right. Emma's growing circle must not depend entirely on whether the player pursues her.
- The goal is not constant tenderness, but an earned sense of **belonging**: Emma begins to think in terms of *us* rather than automatically *me against everyone*.

### 3. The beginning of fun — leisure, art, humor, and student life

As her position becomes more secure and her friendships grow, Emma discovers that not every hour must be spent advancing her career or defending her future. She has always possessed wit, curiosity, and a love of art; she is now increasingly free to enjoy them.

- Make room for music and violin, ballet, books, outings in town, frivolous purchases, social gatherings, excursions, friendly contests, and small comic disasters.
- Let Emma occasionally initiate an activity purely because it will be pleasurable, with no scholarly pretext or strategic benefit.
- Use unplanned, unproductive afternoons as meaningful narrative rewards for someone who has habitually valued productivity above all else.
- Show imperfections and mundane pleasures without making her incompetent or erasing her hard-earned skills.
- Leisure can lead back into the other threads: a concert brings a professional opportunity, a pleasant day out leads to a confidence, or a playful argument inspires a new enchantment.

These are **mutually reinforcing**, not sequential: friendship can make leisure possible before financial security is complete; professional achievement may give Emma the confidence to be vulnerable; an afternoon of fun may create trust that later helps during a serious disagreement.

## Relationship development and voice across Act 2

**Emma becomes more proactive.** The shift from Antonio earning her attention to Emma voluntarily seeking him out is itself a major payoff. She may invite him to browse books, request his company rather than his labor, visit to share a joke, ask his judgment, arrange a match, or simply make time for him on a busy day. She need not always admit why.

**Her behavior changes gradually and subtly, not through a personality replacement.** She retains her incisive intelligence, exacting standards, independence, ambition, competitive playfulness, and banter. Alongside those traits, she increasingly permits warmth, concern, generosity, candid appreciation, and occasional unguarded silences. A brief sincere compliment or instinctive gesture can matter more than a grand confession. Avoid turning every sarcastic line into flirtation, or treating every personal opening as romance.

**Rivalry remains a form of closeness.** Friendly competition and intellectual challenge can increase rivalry alongside friendship, respect, or attraction. Antonio may become her most demanding critic, and therefore one of the people whose sincere praise matters most. Betrayal, deliberate humiliation, and cruelty remain distinct from affectionate rivalry.

**Romantic feelings may emerge, but Act 2 does not formalize a couple.** Emma might recognize attraction in herself while being hesitant to act on it or make commitments. A kiss, an unusually intimate dance, or an impulsive romantic moment may occur in some playthroughs without automatically establishing a relationship. Antonio's feelings and commitments remain subject to player choice. Love is not a required outcome of greater friendship, loyalty, or professional intimacy.

## Independent progression and micro-events

Emma's story **must continue even when Antonio ignores her**. For major strands, design a lightweight *micro-event* or offscreen resolution as the passive counterpart to an event in which Antonio participates: what Emma attempts, achieves, loses, or learns in his absence. These need not be illustrated full scenes or require direct player choices.

- Independent developments should update factual story state (for example, played at a concert, accepted a paid commission, found a solution to an expense, spent an afternoon with Molly) and, where warranted, her circumstances or skills.
- Subsequent full events can use conditional passages to acknowledge whether Antonio was involved or only heard about the result. Other NPCs and ordinary conversation may relay the news naturally: “By the way, did you hear Emma played at the concert?”
- Avoid presenting the player's absence as automatic failure, and avoid retroactively placing Antonio in scenes he did not attend.
- Participation should meaningfully influence the **route, cost, consequences, shared memories, and relationship** rather than being required for Emma to have a life.
- Micro-events should not automatically award Antonio relationship gains for things he never did. They may, however, update Emma's situation and unlock later encounters.
- Maintain clear, mutually exclusive bookkeeping where an attended event and its offscreen counterpart describe alternate versions of the same occurrence; do not allow both to claim incompatible outcomes.

## Milestones and possible Act 2 endpoints

Use **qualitative relationship tags** for significant changes, unlocked by different combinations of accumulated relationship values and meaningful experiences. Prefer multiple possible qualifying histories over one mandatory event chain. A milestone should be **demonstrated in a scene** or a clear, observed change in behavior, rather than appearing as a bare numerical threshold.

Possible provisional milestones include:

- **`emma_trusted_companion`** — post-Falkenheim starting milestone: Emma values Antonio personally, beyond tournament utility.
- **`emma_intellectual_equal`** — she values his judgment and challenges, including when they contradict her own.
- **`emma_personal_confidant`** — she willingly shares genuine concerns and trusts him with parts of herself she usually keeps guarded.
- **`emma_chosen_ally`** — their loyalty and reliability are proven when cooperation has a personal cost.
- **`emma_romantic_tension`** — optional mutual attraction is apparent or acknowledged, without a defined romantic partnership.

These are **examples, not a required set or order**. Relationship metrics remain hidden flexible prerequisites, not player-visible affection bars; factual tags record individual experiences and decisions. Use cross-thread callbacks and conditional passages to make each playthrough's combination of experiences feel specific.

Unlike Act 1, **Act 2 has no single mandatory capstone and no mutually exclusive ending route**. Its possible culminating developments are ways Emma openly acknowledges that Antonio has a lasting place in her life:

- **Long-term professional or business partner:** she sees him as someone with whom she wants to build a future in artificery.
- **Permanent or preferred teammate:** she wants them to continue competing and accomplishing ambitious things together.
- **Best friend and confidant:** she no longer feels obliged to face the world alone and chooses him as one of her closest people.

More than one of these may be true at once. Love, if present, may be understood and felt but remains unformalized; an established romantic relationship belongs to a later stage. Emma's broader transition—from constant defensive struggle toward greater security, belonging, and enjoyment—remains meaningful even in playthroughs that do not reach every Antonio-specific milestone.

## Act 2 event-design guardrails

- Keep mini-arcs **interconnected but optional**, with varied entry conditions, callbacks, and consequences; avoid hard branching into romance, rivalry, and friendship tracks.
- Give important events choices with trade-offs, including cards, skills, resources, professional opportunities, and effects on other characters, not only relationship points.
- Let Emma initiate a growing share of encounters, while respecting Antonio's ability to decline and spend time elsewhere.
- Keep light and funny episodes alongside serious ones; allow sincere moments to land without obligatory punchlines.
- Do not frame financial independence as a reward Antonio grants, romance as the prize for accumulated respect, or leisure as proof Emma has abandoned ambition.
- Use the Falkenheim return journey as an emotional transition: its tournament-result callbacks matter, but neither victory nor defeat blocks the trusted-companion milestone.
